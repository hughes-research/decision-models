# Code of conduct

This page sets out how people are expected to treat each other in this project, how to report a problem privately, and what maintainers do about it. It applies to everyone who takes part: contributors, maintainers, people who open issues, and people who use the demo in a talk or a class.

## Our standard

This project adopts the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) as its code of conduct. The Covenant is the full text and wins wherever this page is shorter or less precise. The rest of this page summarizes it in plain terms and explains how it works here.

The goal is simple: anyone should be able to take part in this project, ask a question, or disagree with a decision without being harassed, belittled or excluded, whatever their background, identity or level of experience.

## Expected behavior

When you take part in this project, you:

- Treat other people with respect and patience, including people who are new to Tetris, decision models, heuristics or operations research.
- Argue about ideas, code and results, not about people. Back a claim with a test, a benchmark or a line in [experiments/RESULTS.md](experiments/RESULTS.md) where you can.
- Accept review and correction gracefully, and give it kindly and specifically.
- Own your mistakes, apologize to the people they affected, and learn from them.
- Respect privacy. Share no one's personal details, and keep personal or machine-specific data out of issues, logs and commits.
- Think about the community as a whole, not only about winning the current discussion.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not accepted in any project space:

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, including unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Insults, slurs, personal or political attacks, and trolling.
- Sharing someone's private details, such as their home or email address, without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews or talks.
- Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in every project space, including:

- the repository itself: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, commit messages and comments in code;
- any chat, forum or other channel set up for the project;
- talks, lectures and workshops where the demo is presented, when people interact as part of the project.

It also applies when you represent the project in public, for example when you post as a maintainer or present the project at an event.

## Report a problem

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it privately. **Do not use a public issue, pull request or discussion to report someone.**

1. Open the repository on its host and go to its security page (on GitHub, the **Security** tab).
2. Choose **Report a vulnerability**. This opens a private advisory that only you and the maintainers can see. The form is designed for security reports, but it is also this project's private channel for conduct reports.
3. Start the title with "Code of conduct report" and include:
   - what happened, and where (links to the issue, comment or pull request help);
   - when it happened;
   - who was involved, if you know;
   - anything else that helps the maintainers understand the situation.

If private reporting is not turned on for the repository, open a public issue that asks for a private contact channel. Put no details of the incident in that issue.

For content that breaks the hosting platform's own rules, you can also use the platform's report feature (on GitHub, **Report content** on a comment or issue). Those reports go to the platform, not to the maintainers.

### What happens next

The maintainers read every report and reply to you in the private advisory. This is a personal research project, so replies come as time allows, with no service-level commitment. The maintainers:

- keep the reporter's identity and the details of the report private, and share them only as far as needed to act on it;
- review the report and any related history before deciding what to do;
- step aside from handling a report that involves them, if another maintainer is available.

Reporting in good faith never leads to action against the reporter.

## Enforcement

The maintainers are responsible for applying this code of conduct. They may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break it, and they explain moderation decisions when that is appropriate.

The Covenant's enforcement guidelines set out four levels of response. The maintainers use them as a guide and choose the level that fits the impact of the behavior:

| Level | Typical cause | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correction | Inappropriate language or other behavior that is unprofessional or unwelcome | A private, written explanation of what was wrong and why. The maintainers may ask for a public apology. |
| Warning | A violation in a single incident or a series of actions | A warning that continuing will lead to further consequences, and a set period of no contact with the people involved. |
| Temporary ban | A serious violation, or sustained inappropriate behavior | A ban from all interaction with the project for a set period. |
| Permanent ban | A pattern of violations, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of groups of people | A permanent ban from all interaction with the project. |
